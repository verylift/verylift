# Running a challenge

A challenge is a time-boxed strength challenge you run with a group of
friends. The creator picks the timeframe and the lifts; each participant
then builds their own goal chart when they join, so everyone is scored
against their own targets. Challenges are invite-only: people join through a
link you share with them. This page walks through the whole lifecycle — from
setting one up, to inviting people, to wrapping it up at the end.

## The lifecycle at a glance

A challenge moves through a few stages:

**draft → active → completed**, or it can be **cancelled** at any point
before it's completed.

- **Draft** — created but not yet started. The leaderboard isn't live yet.
- **Active** — running, and scoring is happening.
- **Completed** — the challenge has ended and the final standings are
  locked in for good.
- **Cancelled** — voided, with no final scoring. Also locked, but with
  nothing to show for it.

Once a challenge is completed or cancelled, nothing more gets scored — the
results are frozen.

## Creating a challenge

Creating a challenge is a short guided wizard:

1. **Name** — pick something your group will recognize.
2. **Dates** — a start date and an end date. Scoring can apply
   retroactively from the start date once someone joins.
3. **Lifts** — choose from every Liftosaur-supported lift (a searchable
   list, with a pinned Popular group of classic barbell lifts up top). This
   list applies to every participant for the whole challenge — each person
   builds their own targets for these lifts when they join.

Finishing the wizard creates the challenge and drops you on a **share
screen** carrying its invite link, ready to copy and send — see
[Invites](#invites) below.

New challenges start out as **drafts**, and the point-eligible window
defaults to counting from the challenge's start date — see
[History window](#counting-history-from-when-you-join-or-from-the-start)
below for what that means and how to change it from Settings mid-challenge.

## Invites

Every challenge is invite-only, and every challenge has exactly one live
**invite link**. Anyone holding that link can join — there's no separate
per-person invitation to send, accept, or decline.

- The link is created with the challenge and shown to you on the share
  screen straight after the wizard. You can always find it again on the
  challenge's **Settings** page.
- Links **expire at the end of the challenge's end date** by default (the
  owner can set a custom expiry/use-cap instead).
- The owner can generate a fresh link at any time from Settings, as long as
  the challenge hasn't ended yet. Doing so immediately **invalidates the old
  one**, which is how you cut off a link that's gotten out further than you
  meant.
- Opening the link takes you straight into the challenge: you're added as a
  participant and sent on to goal setup (see below). A brand-new visitor with
  no account can register through the link and land in the same join flow.
- The link works on a challenge that's still a **draft**, so you can line
  your group up before the challenge starts.

## Picking your goal and locking it in

Before any of your lifts can be scored, you have to complete **goal
setup** — a one-time step, done separately by every participant. You pick
one of three ways to build your chart:

- **Strength standards** — pull published FitnessVolt numbers for a
  population, sex, bodyweight, and tier, and use them as your starting
  targets.
- **Suggested from history** — the app proposes targets based on your own
  recent Liftosaur lift history, with a bit of stretch built in.
- **Fully custom** — type in your own target for each lift, from scratch.

Whichever method you pick prefills the same editable chart — one target
per lift, per rep count from 1 to 10 — and you can adjust any number before
confirming. Any lift with no usable history or standard needs an explicit
number from you; the app never guesses one for you.

That chart is **permanent** — once you confirm it, it can't be changed for
the rest of the challenge. If you try to revisit goal setup afterward, it
just takes you back to the challenge page. If you skip goal setup entirely,
none of your lifts get scored.

Two things happen the moment you confirm your chart:

1. Your past workouts get scored right away against this challenge, so
   the leaderboard reflects your chart immediately — no waiting on a sync.
2. If you're the **creator** and the challenge is still a draft,
   confirming your own goal setup is what **starts the challenge** —
   it flips from draft to active.

## Counting history: from when you join, or from the start

Every challenge scores only the workouts that fall within a window, and
there are two ways that window can be set up:

- **From when you join** — only your workouts logged after the moment you
  joined count. If you haven't joined yet, nothing counts for you yet.
- **From the challenge's start** (the default for new challenges) —
  everyone's window opens on the challenge's start date, no matter when
  they personally joined. Workouts from before you joined, going back to the
  start date, still count.

The challenge's creator (or staff) can change this at any point from the
challenge's **Settings** page — for example, switching to "from when you
join" mid-challenge so a newly invited friend isn't credited with history
from before they were even asked to join.

## Leaving a challenge

If you need to step away from a challenge partway through, you can
**bail** out. Doing so locks in your points as they stand — nothing further
gets scored for you after that, and you drop off the live leaderboard and
points chart. You can't bail from a challenge that has already completed.

## Removing a participant

The owner (or a site admin) can remove someone from a challenge, from the
**Settings** page. It works like bailing on their behalf: their points are
frozen where they stand and their past entries stay on the leaderboard.

Removal is **permanent** — a removed participant can't rejoin, and the
challenge's invite link won't let them back in either.

## Closing a challenge

The creator can close an active challenge early, or it closes
automatically once its end date passes.

When a challenge closes:

1. Everyone still in it gets one last check for new workouts, followed by a
   final scoring pass, so nobody's results are stale at the finish line. If
   this hiccups for one person, it doesn't stop the rest of the challenge
   from closing properly.
2. The challenge is marked **completed** and the standings are locked in.
3. Everyone who was part of the challenge — including anyone who bailed
   earlier — gets notified that it's wrapped up.

Closing an already-completed challenge doesn't do anything further.

## Cancelling a challenge

The creator (or a site admin, if needed) can cancel a challenge that
hasn't already completed or been cancelled.

Cancelling is different from closing: there's no final check for new
workouts, no final scoring, and no notifications sent. It simply locks the
challenge with no results to show. Use closing when you want to wrap up a
challenge and keep the final standings; use cancelling when you want to
call the whole thing off.
